This week, our team made steady progress on our project titled Synesthetic Learning: Modeling
Cross-Modal Emotional Resonance Between Audio and Abstract Art Using Deep Learning. We focused
on strengthening the foundation of the study, clarifying the research direction, and organizing the major
sections needed for the initial draft. The project aims to understand how emotional meaning can connect
audio and abstract visual forms, and how deep learning can capture these shared affective patterns across
sensory domains.
Throughout the week, we worked together to build the conceptual base of the research. My main
contribution involved refining the structure of the study and shaping how each section connects to the
larger goal. I helped organize the flow of the report by identifying the key ideas that link sound, emotion,
and abstract art. I also reviewed and reworked several parts of the narrative so the problem context would
be easier to follow and more consistent with our aim of modeling emotional correspondence.
I contributed to strengthening the research significance by examining existing work in multimodal and
affective learning. I pointed out how many current models remain focused on only one type of input and
how few attempts have been made to align emotional meaning across different senses. This helped the
team build a clearer argument on why cross-modal emotional modeling is both relevant and needed in
emerging fields of AI.
For this week’s written output, I assisted in developing several structural components of the report. I
worked on the flow of the introduction and helped refine the section that discusses emotional perception
in art and audio. I also supported the creation of the objectives by reviewing how they could reflect both
the technical goals and the interpretive aspects of the study. In addition, I suggested clearer wording for
parts of the significance section so the practical impact on creativity, design, and emotional experience
would be more understandable.
I also contributed to outlining the expected results of the model. I helped describe how the shared
representation space, clustering patterns, and emotional mapping will indicate whether the model
successfully forms cross-modal connections. This gave the team a more concrete vision of what our
evaluations will capture in the next stages.
