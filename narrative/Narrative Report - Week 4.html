This week focused on comparing several model architectures for the audio emotion classifier and
improving the strongest one through hyperparameter tuning. Since the preprocessing pipeline was already
in place, the goal was to identify which model handled Mel-spectrogram inputs most effectively and
determine the configuration that would carry forward into the cross-modal stages of the project.
I helped evaluate a set of models that included CNN-based, hybrid recurrent, and transformer-style
architectures. After running them under the same training conditions, the simple CNN performed best,
reaching the highest validation accuracy while remaining the most efficient to train. More complex
models like ResNet and the hybrid CNN-LSTM variants took much longer and showed no meaningful
improvements, confirming that convolutional features were the best fit for our dataset.
Once the CNN was selected, I used Optuna to run multiple tuning trials. The search tested a range of
learning rates, batch sizes, and regularization values. The best trial settled on a moderately small learning
rate and a batch size of 16, resulting in a tuned model that reached a validation accuracy a little above
0.82. Class performance was balanced overall, with the model doing particularly well on Sad samples.
To check consistency, I trained the optimized CNN for a full set of epochs and reviewed its learning
curves, confusion matrix, and classification metrics. The results confirmed that the model was stable and
ready for the next part of the system.
By the end of the week, I also prepared the initial structure for the conditional GAN that will later use the
classifier’s embeddings. Early training steps showed good loss behavior, indicating that the adversarial
setup is functioning properly.
